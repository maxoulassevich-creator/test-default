Live Casino in Kenya: How Dealer Games Work

In a live casino the cards are dealt by a person in a studio and streamed to your screen, while the betting happens through the interface in front of you. This guide covers the formats, how a round is timed, what table limits and a dropped connection actually mean — and why the pace is the part worth planning for.

Section 01

What Is Live Casino

A real table, filmed. Everything you do still goes through software.

A live casino game runs in a studio with a physical table and a human dealer. Cameras send the video to your device; your stakes, your decisions and your balance travel back through the same interface used everywhere else on the site. The result comes from what happens on the table — a card turned over, a ball settling in a pocket — rather than from a random number generator.

That difference is mostly about how the game is presented. The payouts, the house edge and the table's own rules are set before you sit down, and seeing the dealer does not change any of them.

The dealer

A person running the table to a fixed procedure. They deal, announce the round and confirm the result — they do not decide payouts and cannot vary the rules.

The stream

Live video, usually with a choice of quality. A weaker connection lowers the picture first; it does not slow the table down or hold the round for you.

The betting interface

Chips, the table layout and the action buttons, overlaid on the video. Everything you stake is placed here, inside a window that closes on a timer.

Round information

The round number, the countdown, table limits and recent results. Round IDs are what a support query is checked against, so they are worth noting when something looks wrong.

Section 02

Common Live Casino Formats

Four families cover almost every live lobby, whatever the table is branded as.

  • Roulette

    One wheel, many bets. The betting window closes before the ball is released, and payouts follow the bet type.

  • Blackjack

    Hit, stand, split or double against the dealer. Seats can be limited, so tables often fill at peak hours.

  • Baccarat

    Back the player hand, the banker hand or a tie. Drawing rules are fixed, so there is nothing to decide once bets close.

  • Game shows

    Wheel and quiz formats with a presenter. Rules and multipliers are specific to each show and change between them.

Section 03

At the Table: Round, Limits and Connection

Every live round runs to the same four beats, on the table's clock rather than yours.

  1. Betting window

    A countdown opens. Chips can be placed, changed or cleared while it runs.

  2. Bets closed

    The timer ends and the table locks. Anything not confirmed by then is not in the round.

  3. The deal

    The dealer plays the round out on camera — cards turned, wheel spun, result announced.

  4. Settlement

    Winning bets are paid to the balance and the round is logged with its own ID.

Table limits are shown before you join and set the smallest and largest stake that table accepts. They exist per bet type, not per table alone: a roulette table can take a small stake on red while requiring more on a single number.

The timer is the part that catches people out. It runs whether or not you are ready, and a round you did not confirm in time simply happens without you. Tables advertised as "speed" or "turbo" shorten that window deliberately.

If the connection drops, the round continues — the table is real and does not pause for one player. Bets already confirmed stay in and are settled to your balance; decisions still owed, such as whether to draw another card in blackjack, are resolved by the table's published rules.

Check before you sit down

  • Minimum and maximum stake for the bet you intend to place
  • How long the betting window stays open
  • What the rules say about a lost connection
  • Whether side bets are offered and how they are paid
  • Where the round history and round IDs are shown

All of it sits in the table's information panel, usually behind an i icon next to the table name.

A dropped connection is not a cancelled bet. Once the betting window has closed, the stake is committed. If a round looks wrong, note the round ID and the time before contacting support — that is what any check is run against.

Section 04

Live and Automated Games Compared

The same game exists in both forms. What changes is who runs it and how fast it moves.

Live dealer tables and automated games compared
  Live table Automated game
How the round runs Physically, on camera In software, on request
Who deals A dealer following fixed procedure A random number generator
Pace Set by the table's countdown Set by you — as fast as you click
Interface Chips overlaid on live video Fully rendered game screen
Availability Studio hours, and seats can fill Always open, no seat needed

Neither form is the better bet. The payouts and the house edge are properties of the game's rules, not of how the result is produced — a double-zero roulette wheel costs the same whether a dealer spins it or software does. What genuinely differs is the rhythm, and with it how many rounds an hour contains.

Section 05

Live Casino Risks and Session Limits

A live table never ends. The next countdown starts on its own, and staying seated takes no decision at all.

That is the specific risk of this format: the pace is continuous, the dealer is sociable and the interface hides the clock. Set a session limit in the account before you join a table, not a mental one, and decide the amount you are willing to lose beforehand. Leaving between rounds is easy; leaving mid-streak never is.

FAQ

Live Casino FAQ

How is live casino different from an automated game?

The round is played out physically by a dealer and streamed to you, instead of being generated by software. The betting still happens through the interface, and the payouts and house edge come from the game's rules either way. The real difference is pace: a live table runs on its own countdown, an automated game runs at your speed.

What happens if my connection drops mid-round?

The round carries on — the table is real and does not wait. Bets confirmed before the window closed stay in and are settled to your balance. Any decision still owed, such as hitting or standing in blackjack, is resolved under the table's published rules. Those rules are in the table information panel.

What are table limits?

The smallest and largest stake a table accepts, shown before you join. They apply per bet type rather than to the table as a whole, so an outside bet in roulette and a straight-up number can carry different minimums at the same table.

How long is the betting window?

It varies by table and is displayed as a countdown — commonly a few seconds in speed formats and longer at standard tables. When it reaches zero the table locks, and a bet not confirmed by then is not part of the round.

Can I check a round afterwards?

Yes. Live tables log every round with an ID and keep a visible history, and your account keeps a record of the bets placed. If something looks wrong, note the round ID and the time — support checks the query against that record.
