Betting Bonuses: Understanding Terms and Conditions

Betting bonuses are credited under conditions, and the conditions are where the value actually sits. This page explains how promotional funds differ from your own money, what a wagering requirement asks of you, and which four terms decide whether an offer is worth accepting. It lists no offers and recommends none.

On this page What they are Common types Wagering requirements Terms to check Offers & responsible play FAQ

Section 01

What Are Betting Bonuses

A bonus is credit with strings attached, held separately from the money you deposited.

A betting bonus is an amount an operator adds to your account under published conditions. It is not the same thing as your own funds: until those conditions are met it usually cannot be withdrawn, and in many cases it cannot be spent freely either. Accounts therefore run two balances at once, and knowing which one a stake is coming out of is the whole skill.

Cash balance

Your own money — deposits and settled winnings. It can be staked on anything the operator offers and withdrawn whenever you choose.

Promotional balance

Bonus credit. Usually restricted to certain games or markets, subject to a wagering requirement, and not withdrawable until that requirement is met.

Eligibility

Who may claim, and how. Typically one per person, household, device or payment method, and often requiring a verified account or an opt-in before depositing.

Opting in usually has to happen first. A deposit made before the offer is accepted often cannot be credited retrospectively, and support is rarely able to override it. If an offer interests you, read its terms before adding funds, not after.

Section 02

Common Types

The names are close to standard across operators, even where the terms behind them are not.

  • Welcome bonus

    Credited on a first deposit, usually as a percentage of it. Carries the heaviest conditions of any offer type.

  • Free bet

    A stake credited for one bet. The stake itself is normally not returned with a win — only the profit is.

  • Free spins

    Rounds on named slots at a fixed stake value. Winnings from them usually land as bonus funds, not cash.

  • Cashback

    A share of net losses returned over a set period. Check whether it arrives as cash or as bonus credit — the difference is large.

  • Reload bonus

    A smaller repeat offer on later deposits, often limited to particular days, payment methods or deposit sizes.

  • Loyalty rewards

    Points accrued from staking and exchanged for credit. The exchange rate, not the points total, is what matters.

Section 03

Wagering Requirements

The single term that decides what a bonus is worth. It is arithmetic, and it is worth doing before accepting.

A wagering requirement is the total amount that must be staked before bonus funds — and anything won with them — become withdrawable. It is written as a multiple: 10× or 30×, applied either to the bonus alone or to the bonus plus the deposit that triggered it. Which of the two the offer uses can double the work, so it is the first line to read.

Turnover counts the stake, not the loss. Placing KSh 1 000 and getting KSh 900 back contributes the full KSh 1 000 to the requirement, and the KSh 900 can be staked again. That is why the totals look reachable — and why they take far more rounds than the multiple suggests, with the house edge applying to every one of them.

Illustrative example
DepositKSh 1 000
Bonus creditedKSh 1 000
Wagering requirement20× bonus
Total to stake before withdrawalKSh 20 000
If applied to bonus and depositKSh 40 000

Figures are chosen to show the arithmetic and describe no actual offer. The same multiple produces two very different requirements depending on one word in the terms.

Not every stake counts the same. Offers weight contributions by game: slots often count in full, table games a fraction, live tables sometimes nothing at all. Sports offers do the same with a minimum odds rule, so selections priced below it add nothing to the total. Both are stated in the terms, usually as a table.

Section 04

Terms to Check, with an Example

Four lines decide whether an offer is worth taking. Everything else is presentation.

The requirement and what it applies to

The multiple, and whether it is calculated on the bonus alone or on bonus plus deposit. This one number sets the size of everything else.

Eligible games, markets and odds

Which games count toward the requirement and at what weighting, or for sports, the minimum odds a selection must carry to count at all.

The time limit

How long you have before unmet bonus funds expire — commonly days rather than weeks. An unrealistic window makes a large offer worth less than a small one.

Maximum bet and withdrawal cap

The largest stake permitted while wagering, and any ceiling on what can be withdrawn from bonus winnings. Exceeding the stake cap can void the bonus outright.

Common bonus clauses and what they mean in practice
Clause as written What it means in practice
"30× wagering on bonus and deposit" Both amounts are multiplied, so the total to stake is twice what the multiple first suggests.
"Slots 100%, table games 10%" KSh 1 000 staked on a table game moves the requirement by KSh 100. The same stake on a slot moves it by KSh 1 000.
"Minimum odds 1.50" Selections priced below 1.50 contribute nothing, so short-priced bets do not reduce the requirement at all.
"Maximum bet KSh 500 while wagering" A single larger stake can void the bonus and everything won with it, even accidentally.
"Winnings capped at 5× bonus" Anything above the cap is removed at withdrawal, however the balance was built.
"Valid for 7 days" The clock runs from crediting, not from first use, and unmet funds are removed when it expires.

Clauses above are written in the style operators use and are examples, not quotations from any offer. The terms that bind you are the ones published with the promotion at the moment you accept it.

Section 05

Offers and Responsible Gambling

A wagering requirement is an instruction to keep playing. That is worth naming plainly, because it is the mechanism.

Offers are designed to extend sessions and raise stakes: turnover targets reward volume, expiry dates add urgency, and both push in the same direction. Accepting one is a decision to play more than you otherwise would, so make it deliberately. Your deposit and time limits should be set before the offer, not adjusted to fit it — and declining an offer is always available, at no cost.

  • Before accepting an offer
  • Work out the total stake the requirement asks for
  • Check the time limit against how you actually play
  • Check the maximum stake and any withdrawal cap
  • Confirm it does not push you past your own limits

FAQ

Bonuses FAQ

What is a wagering requirement?

The total amount that must be staked before bonus funds and their winnings can be withdrawn, written as a multiple such as 20×. Read whether it applies to the bonus alone or to bonus plus deposit — the same multiple then means twice the turnover.

Does every game count toward wagering?

No. Contributions are weighted by game type: slots commonly count in full, table games a fraction, and some live tables not at all. Sports offers use minimum odds instead, so a selection priced below the stated figure adds nothing. The weighting table is part of the terms.

Can bonus money be withdrawn?

Not until the requirement is met. Bonus credit sits in a separate promotional balance, and until it is released it cannot be cashed out. Many offers also cap what can be withdrawn from bonus winnings, with anything above the cap removed at withdrawal.

What voids a bonus?

Most often exceeding the maximum stake allowed while wagering, letting the time limit expire, requesting a withdrawal before the requirement is met, or playing an excluded game. The consequence is usually the loss of the bonus and everything won with it, so the stake cap is worth knowing exactly.

Is a free bet the same as free money?

No. A free bet stakes an amount on your behalf, and if it wins you generally receive the profit only — the stake is not returned with it. A KSh 500 free bet at odds of 3.00 therefore returns KSh 1 000, not KSh 1 500. Minimum odds and eligible markets usually apply too.
